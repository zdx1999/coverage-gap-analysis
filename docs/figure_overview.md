# Figure overview

## Figure 1

National study system and analytical framework. The figure introduces the leave-one-event-out definition of historical hotspot inventories, the social-media flood-report processing workflow, and the national spatial basis of the flood-report layer.

## Figure 2

Historical inventories cover only a minority of event-time reported impacts. Representative city-events and event-level statistics show that uncovered reported-impact footprints are widespread across extreme rainfall events.

## Figure 3

Coverage gaps are linked to historical-record depth and urban structure. Standardized model associations test whether coverage gaps are more strongly related to historical inventory structure, urban activity and exposure, physical context, or event intensity.

## Figure 4

Exposure outside historical hotspots is large, concentrated and robust. The figure quantifies population, road and urban-function exposure outside historical hotspot inventories, evaluates concentration across cities, and reports robustness checks.

## Figure 5

Coverage gaps identify city priorities beyond historical hotspots. City rankings translate uncovered impact space and outside exposure into update priorities under observed extreme events and a tail-rainfall stress-test scenario.
